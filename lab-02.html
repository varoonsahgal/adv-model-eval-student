Concepts

6 ideas, defined where you need them

Each one gets three sentences in the notebook: what it is, what it can't tell you, and an anchor from this lab.

  • 📘
    Open codingfor each failing trace, one free-text sentence in your own words on the first thing that went wrong, written before any categories exist ("Said 1099-NEC is due Feb 28; it's Jan 31. Retrieval pulled the old 1099-MISC article").
  • 📘
    Theoretical saturationthe point in a review where new traces stop revealing new failure modes. Plot distinct modes seen against traces read and look for the flat part; a common stopping rule is 20 traces in a row with nothing new.
  • 📘
    Adjusted Rand index (ARI)a score for how well two groupings of the same items agree. For every pair of items, do both groupings treat it the same way (together in both, or apart in both)? The share of agreeing pairs is the Rand index; ARI rescales it so 1.0 = identical groupings and 0 = no better than chance (it can dip below 0). Cluster numbers don't matter, only who is grouped with whom.
  • 📘
    Root-cause layerthe layer where the fix goes: prompt, retrieval, tool, model, or harness (your own logging, eval, or test setup). The expert recorded one per failing trace while open-coding (the root_cause column).
  • 📘
    Harnesseverything you built around the product to record or grade it: the tracing exporter, the trace store, the eval graders, the test environment. A harness bug manufactures "failures" that no customer experienced.
  • 📘
    Impact scorefor each failure mode, the sum of a severity weight over its traces (low 1 · medium 2 · high 3 · critical 5), which is the same as frequency × average severity. The weights are a team decision: write them down and revisit them.

Words you’ll meet

Jargon decoder

The notebook defines each term right before you need it. Here’s the cheat sheet.

Trace
The logged record of one conversation: user message, tool calls and their results, final response
Span
One timed step inside a trace (a model call, a tool call). Its attributes are fields such as the response text
Automated check
A code rule run on every trace (non-empty, no card numbers, polite, short)
Capability suite vs regression suite
Checks for what the system can't yet do reliably (healthy near 70% pass) vs checks that guard what already works (near 100%, gate CI)
Stratified sample
A sample deliberately over-drawn from some group (here, thumbs-downs), so its failure rate overstates production
Open coding
A free-text note on the first thing that went wrong in a trace, written before any categories exist
First upstream failure
The earliest step that went wrong. Later errors usually follow from it
Benevolent dictator
The one domain expert whose labeling call is final, so labels stay consistent
Cohen's κ (kappa)
Agreement between two raters after subtracting the agreement chance alone would produce
Criteria drift
Your pass/fail criteria change as you read outputs (Shankar et al., 2024), so you can't write them all up front
Axial coding
Grouping open codes that share a mechanism into named failure modes
Failure mode · taxonomy · codebook
A named, app-specific way the assistant fails · the full set of modes · the set written down with a definition each
All 40 terms in this lab
Failure family
One of six generic categories (boundary, unsupported claim, incomplete, tool misuse, planning, execution): a coverage checklist
Theoretical saturation
The point where new traces stop revealing new failure modes
Prevalence (p)
How often a failure mode occurs per trace (1% = 1 trace in 100)
Embedding · vector
A list of numbers (here 384) that represents a text's meaning; similar meanings get similar numbers
Cosine similarity
How closely two vectors point the same way: 1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated
K-Means · k · centroid
Clustering into k groups, each around its centroid (the average of its members)
Seed (random_state)
The number that fixes a random start, so every run gives the same result
Facet · failure summary
One LLM-written sentence about one aspect of a trace (here: how it failed)
Clio
Anthropic's system for analyzing conversations at scale: LLM facet → embed → K-Means → LLM-named clusters → human review
Rand index · ARI
The share of item pairs two groupings treat the same way (together in both, or apart in both). Adjusted rescales it so chance = 0 and identical = 1
Purity
The share of items that match their cluster's most common label
Root-cause layer
Where the fix goes: prompt, retrieval, tool, model, or harness
Retrieval
The step that searches the help center (search_help) for articles to ground the answer
Harness
Your own logging, tracing, eval, and test setup: everything around the product that records or grades it
OpenTelemetry (OTel) · exporter
The standard tracing library · its component that ships spans to the trace store (and may cap each attribute's length)
Docent · InterCode · Cybench
Transluce's transcript-analysis tool · an interactive-coding agent benchmark · a cybersecurity capture-the-flag agent benchmark
Severity weight · impact
A team-chosen number per severity level · the sum of those weights over a mode's traces
Hard gate
A failure that blocks release on a single occurrence, whatever its rank
Wilson confidence interval
The range of true pass rates consistent with what you saw in n cases; reliable even for small n
1099-NEC
The IRS form a business files to report what it paid an independent contractor. Due January 31
W-2 · W-9
An employee's annual wage statement (due to employees January 31) · the form a contractor fills in to give you their tax ID
Form 941
The employer's quarterly federal payroll tax return
Worker classification
Deciding whether someone is an employee or a contractor. It changes who owes which taxes, so it's a handoff topic
Sales-tax nexus
A connection with a state (such as enough sales there) that obliges you to register and collect its sales tax
HST
Canada's harmonized sales tax, used in Ontario. Not a US state sales tax
Cash vs accrual basis
Two ways to keep books: record income when you're paid, or when you invoice
S-corp
A US business structure whose owner-employees must pay themselves a reasonable salary through payroll
Bad debt · P&L · reconcile
An invoice you'll never collect · profit-and-loss statement · match your books to the bank statement

🏆 Challenges

5 problems that need thinking, not typing

Each one is built around a point you discover by solving it. Starters run as shipped; 🌶️ is the spice level.

  1. 🌶️level 1
    Challenge 1: Break the cheap checkA monitor that false-alarms on ordinary fintech text gets muted within a week, and a muted monitor catches nothing.
  2. 🌶️🌶️level 2
    Challenge 2: Derive the reading budget for a rare incidentIf your review budget can't see a 1-in-200 incident, the first you hear of it is from the customer whose invoice went to a stranger.
  3. 🌶️🌶️🌶️level 3
    Challenge 3: A harness-cap detector that generalizesA detector that only recognizes last month's bug waves the next one through, and a logging cap turns back into a model-upgrade ticket.
  4. 🌶️🌶️level 2
    Challenge 4: Which half of the sample should you report?A confident wrong tax answer gets a thumbs-up; the customer finds out at filing time, and by then a review that samples only the complaints has already looked past it.
  5. 🌶️🌶️🌶️level 3
    Challenge 5: How lucky was trace 31?Task 4's stopping rule looked reasonable because you saw one review order; a rule tuned on one ordering goes into the team's playbook and silently sets the miss rate of every cycle after it.
