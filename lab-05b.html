Words you’ll meet

Jargon decoder

The notebook defines each term right before you need it. Here’s the cheat sheet.

Golden set
A fixed test set of questions with expert-graded answers, re-run on every version
LLM judge
A language model prompted to grade another model's answer PASS or FAIL
GA (general availability)
Releasing to every customer, not a beta slice
Enrolled agent ("the tax pro")
A federally licensed US tax professional; the ground truth in this lab
Point estimate
One number (78%) with no error bar
Standard error (SE)
The typical size of an estimate's wobble from one sample of questions to the next
Confidence interval (CI), coverage
A range from a recipe that captures the truth in 95% of repeated samples; that 95% is its coverage
Wilson interval
A CI for pass rates that stays honest at small n and near 0% or 100%
Null (null hypothesis)
The "nothing changed" world you test against
p-value
In the null world, the chance of a result at least this extreme. Not the chance the null is true
Two-sided
Counts extreme results in both directions (better or worse)
Paired vs unpaired
Comparing the same questions one by one vs comparing two separate pass rates
All 40 terms in this lab
Discordant pairs (fixed / broke)
Questions whose PASS/FAIL outcome changed between versions
McNemar test
A coin-flip test on the fixed vs broke split
Correlation (r)
How much two things move together, from −1 to 1; 0 = unrelated
Standard deviation (SD), variance, ddof=1
Spread around the mean; variance = SD²; ddof=1 divides by n − 1, the usual choice for a sample
Cluster, clustered SE
A group of correlated cases (one conversation); an SE that sums within each group first
Effective n
How many independent cases a correlated set is really worth
Bootstrap, with replacement
Resample your data (repeats allowed), recompute, repeat; the middle 95% of results is the CI
Nondeterminism
Same input, different output on different runs
Resampling K answers
Asking each question K times and averaging per question
Power, α, β
Power = chance of detecting a real change; α = false-alarm rate; β = miss rate = 1 − power
δ, MDE
The smallest change you care about; the smallest change your set can reliably detect
z-cutoff
Distance from the center of a bell curve in SEs; ±1.96 holds the middle 95%
σ², ω²
Answer-to-answer randomness within a question; true variation of the gain between questions
Positive class
The label treated as "yes" (here PASS)
Confusion matrix (TP, FN, FP, TN)
The 2×2 table of judge vs expert verdicts
TPR, TNR
Share of good answers the judge passes; share of bad answers it catches
Imbalanced labels
One class dominates (here most answers pass)
Cohen's κ (kappa)
Agreement beyond what chance alone would produce; 0 = chance, 1 = perfect
Rogan-Gladen correction
Back-calculates the true pass rate from a judge's rate and its TPR/TNR
Youden's J
TPR + TNR − 1: the share of any real change that survives the judge
Multiple comparisons, family-wise error rate
Testing many metrics; the chance that at least one is a false alarm
Bonferroni, Holm
Corrections that scale p-values by the number of tests
Pre-registration, exploratory
Naming the primary metric before looking; everything else is a lead, not a win
Non-inferiority
Testing that v2 is not worse than v1 by more than a set margin
Delta method
Propagating small input errors through a formula using its derivatives
Tax terms in the data
1099-NEC: IRS form reporting payments to an independent contractor. S-corp / K-1: a corporation whose income passes through to its owners, each told their share on a Schedule K-1. Section 179: deduct equipment in the year you buy it. Safe harbor: estimated-tax payment levels that avoid an underpayment penalty. QBI: the qualified business income deduction for pass-through owners
Structured output, pydantic
Forcing a model's reply into a schema; pydantic declares that schema as a Python class
DROP, MGSM, RACE-H, HealthBench
Public benchmarks cited in research cards: reading + arithmetic, multilingual grade-school math, exam reading comprehension, medical conversations
