Conversations, Not Prompts: Evaluating Multi-Turn Assistants with Simulated Users

Two tax-help assistants. One answers in its first reply and wins every per-turn helpfulness score. One asks for missing facts first. Simulated customers with hidden facts talk to both. Which one gives people the right number?

Words you’ll meet

Jargon decoder

The notebook defines each term right before you need it. Here’s the cheat sheet.

W-2 wages
Salary from an employer, who withholds tax from each paycheck
1099 income
Freelance or contract income; nobody withholds tax from it
Filing status
The category you file under: single, married filing jointly (one return for a couple), or head of household (unmarried and supporting a dependent)
Dependent
A person you support, usually a child, who earns you a tax credit
Standard deduction
A flat amount subtracted from income before tax is computed
Tax bracket
A slice of income taxed at one rate (here 10% on the first $20k, 20% above)
Self-employment tax
Extra tax on 1099 income (here a flat 15%), on top of income tax
Credit vs deduction
A deduction lowers the income that gets taxed; a credit subtracts from the tax itself
Quarterly estimated payments
Freelancers prepay tax four times a year because no employer withholds it
IRS notice
A letter from the US tax agency saying, for example, you owe more
S-corp election
Choosing to have a business taxed as a corporation: a legal and financial decision
Sole proprietor
A one-person business that isn't a separate legal entity
All 34 terms in this lab
Turn
One Penny reply, plus any tool calls behind it
Per-turn judge
An LLM judge that grades one reply at a time, without the hidden facts
Conversation-level grading
One grade for the whole conversation, from its end state
Oracle
Code that computes the right answer from the customer's true facts
Tool-grounded
The number came from a tool call, not the model's own arithmetic
Persona card
A test customer: goal, opening line, hidden facts, chat style
Hidden facts
True facts the customer knows and shares only when asked
User simulator
Code (often an LLM) that plays the customer from a persona card
τ-bench
A public benchmark (Yao et al., 2024) that tests agents against simulated users
Tolerance policy
How far from the oracle still counts as right (here $50)
Clarification recall / precision
Did Penny ask for the facts she needed / were her questions useful?
Lucky pass
A correct outcome the assistant didn't earn
Escalation (handoff)
Passing the conversation to a human expert
Confusion matrix
The 2×2 table of should/shouldn't × did/didn't
Unprompted-leak rate
Share of hidden facts the simulator revealed without being asked
Wilson interval
A 95% confidence range for a rate; wide when n is small
Doom loop
The assistant repeats itself without progress until the customer quits
pass^k / pass@k
All k trials succeed / at least one of k succeeds
TPR / TNR
Share of violations a judge catches / share of clean replies it passes
Cohen's κ (kappa)
Agreement corrected for the agreement you'd get by chance
Hard / soft gate
A launch rule that blocks on its own / one that adds caution
Paired comparison
Two versions run on the same personas and seeds, so only the prompt differs
