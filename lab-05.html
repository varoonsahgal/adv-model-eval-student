Words you’ll meet

Jargon decoder

The notebook defines each term right before you need it. Here’s the cheat sheet.

Earnings summary
A short write-up of a company's quarterly results that investors act on
Revenue · net income · net loss
Sales · profit after every cost · a negative net income
QoQ · YoY
Quarter-over-quarter (vs the previous quarter) · year-over-year (vs the same quarter last year)
EPS
Earnings per share: net income ÷ shares outstanding
Gross · operating margin
Share of revenue left after direct costs · after all operating costs
Guidance
Management's forecast range for next quarter's results
Debt covenant waiver
Lenders agree not to enforce a loan condition the company broke or was about to break. A distress signal
Leverage
Debt relative to what the company can pay it with; this lab's rubric flags debt above 2× cash
Material
Big enough to change a reasonable reader's decision
Claim · atomic claim
One checkable statement · one subject, one metric, one value
Fabrication · distortion · conflation
Three kinds of hallucination: an invented detail · a real fact with wrong details · facts mixed across sources or periods
Factuality vs completeness
"Is what it says true?" vs "Does it say everything material?"
All 34 terms in this lab
Deterministic check
Code that gives the same answer every time (a regex, a recomputation); no model involved
Entailment
Whether a text actually states or implies a fact. Word overlap can't tell "a waiver was obtained" from "no waiver was obtained"; an entailment check can
Absolute · relative error
|stated − actual| · that, divided by |actual|
Percentage points (pp)
The plain difference between two percentages: 25% vs 20% is 5 pp apart, a 25% relative error
Failure mode
One specific, observable way an output goes wrong ("omits a material risk")
Rubric
The written criteria a judge grades against
Likert scale
A 1–5 rating ("how good is it?")
LLM judge
A model given the source, the output and one rubric check, returning a verdict
Structured output · Pydantic
The API forces the reply to match a schema · the Python library used to define that schema as a class
Critique-then-verdict
The judge writes its evidence before its decision, so the decision is conditioned on it
UNKNOWN (abstention)
The judge declines to decide; a human does
Meta-evaluation
Evaluating the evaluator against expert labels
TP · FN · FP · TN
With FAIL as "positive": caught defect · missed defect · false alarm · correct pass
TPR (recall) · TNR (specificity)
Share of real failures the judge catches · share of good outputs it passes
Raw agreement · base rate
Share of verdicts that match · how often a rater says FAIL at all
Cohen's κ (kappa)
Agreement left after subtracting what the two raters' base rates would produce by luck; 0 = chance, 1 = perfect
95% confidence interval (Wilson)
The range of true rates consistent with what you observed; Wilson is the version that behaves on small samples
Dev · test split
Labels you tune a judge on · held-out labels you score it on once
Slicing
Grouping errors by an input feature (format, position, type) to find the pattern
Pairwise judging · position / verbosity / style bias
The judge picks the better of two outputs · it favours the first slot / longer text / markdown formatting, regardless of content
Self-preference · PoLL
A judge favouring its own model family · a "panel of LLM evaluators" that votes
Defense in depth · IRS OPR
Independent layers, so one layer's blind spot is another's catch · the IRS office that regulates tax practitioners
